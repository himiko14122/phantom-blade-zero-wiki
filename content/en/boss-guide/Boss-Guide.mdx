## Early Game Bosses: Coppermaul and Red Wraith

The first two boss encounters double as tutorials for the game's core combat verbs. New players should treat them as a controlled environment to learn timing before the difficulty ramps up.

### Wan Jun Coppermaul

Coppermaul wields a massive warhammer that scatters loose dirt across the arena with every heavy swing. The trick is that the scattered dirt slows your dodge recovery by roughly 0.3 seconds, which punishes panic rolls. The fight has three phases:

- **Phase 1 (100%–60% HP):** Three-hit combos ending in an overhead slam. Parry the first two hits and sidestep the third.
- **Phase 2 (60%–30% HP):** Adds a horizontal sweep that has longer wind-up but tracks further. Wait for the red flash before committing to a parry.
- **Phase 3 (30%–0% HP):** Enters a Breached state after every fourth parry, opening a 4-second punish window. Save your heaviest Sha-Chi combo here.

### Red Wraith

The Red Wraith is a rooftop fight with verticality that the rest of the early game lacks. She teleports between raised platforms and drops down with plunging strikes. Skipping her is technically possible, but doing so reportedly weakens the final battle's opening cutscene buffs. Her most dangerous attack is a five-hit aerial combo that ends with a shockwave — only dodge on the final hit, because mid-combo i-frames won't carry you through the shockwave.

The **Red Wraith** fight also teaches players about Ghostep timing, which is covered in detail in the [Phantom Blade Zero Ghostep guide](/en/guides/ghostep-explained/). Her arena is one of the few places where Ghostep's brief invulnerability window lets you cross long gaps without falling.

## Mid Game Bosses: Seven Stars and TieSha

By Chapter 3, bosses stop tolerating button mashing. The two mid-game encounters test your ability to manage multiple enemies and your patience with long attack chains.

### Chief Disciple of Seven Stars

This is technically a formation fight, not a solo duel. The Chief Disciple spawns six minions that heal him when they die in a specific order — sacrifice the weakest one first, then the strongest, then alternate. The mechanic rewards consistency over panic, because killing the wrong minion locks you out of the heal-sacrifice chain for 12 seconds. The Chief Disciple himself uses a polearm with extended range, so keep distance until the formation breaks.

Boss-specific tips:

- **Formation rule:** Kill minions in the order signaled by the glowing aura above each one. The aura color cycles every 8 seconds.
- **Punish window:** After a successful formation break, the Chief Disciple enters a 6-second Breached state — that's your only chance to combo heavy hits.
- **Healing:** Don't heal during the minion phase. Use the time to reposition.
- **Weapon swap:** Bring a polearm-spear for reach; the **Hollow Bloom** polearm with innate +15% thrust damage is recommended by community testers.

### TieSha the Frenzy

TieSha is a dual-blade berserker whose rage meter climbs every 10 seconds. At max rage, she unlocks a flurry combo that can two-shot you even at full health. The counter is to break her Breached state within the first 8 seconds of every rage phase, which knocks her out of the meter entirely. Her base difficulty is rated ★★★★☆ in this Phantom Blade Zero boss guide because the timing tolerance is roughly 0.15 seconds — tighter than any previous encounter.

Practice the parry timing on training dummies before attempting TieSha, since the [how to parry tutorial](/en/guides/how-to-parry/) breaks down the exact frame-perfect input windows you'll need against her flurry.

## Late Game Bosses: Huangxing and Commander Cleave

The final two bosses represent the mechanical ceiling of the game. Both demand full mastery of dodge, parry, Sha-Chi management, and recovery from the **Breached state** — recovery details are covered in the [Phantom Blade Zero Breached state recovery guide](/en/guides/breached-state-recovery/).

### Huangxing the Sunken

Huangxing fights in a flooded temple arena that rises and falls with water levels. The mechanic is poison: standing in water drains 2% HP per second, and certain attacks flood the entire arena for 4 seconds. The boss uses a heavy cauldron-like weapon that splashes water on every swing, layering the poison effect on top of normal damage.

| Phase | Arena Status | Boss Behavior | Priority |
|-------|--------------|---------------|----------|
| 100%–70% | Water level low | Standard 3-hit combos | Punish with light attacks |
| 70%–40% | Water level rises | Adds poison splash | Stay on platforms, parry splash |
| 40%–0% | Flooded arena | Continuous damage | DPS race, ignore defense |

The **Huangxing** fight is rated ★★★★★ because the flooded arena forces aggressive play — passive players run out of healing supplies within 90 seconds. Bring stackable antidotes instead of basic healing, because antidotes remove the poison DoT while basic heals only restore HP.

### Commander Cleave

The final boss is a frontline war commander with a greatsword and shield. He counters your parry after the first successful use, meaning you must alternate between parry and dodge for the entire fight. His Breached state triggers automatically after you deplete his stamina (separate from HP), which gives you roughly 8 seconds of free damage. The **Commander Cleave** fight punishes players who rely on a single defensive tool, since the moment you commit to all-parry or all-dodge, he'll switch to an attack that beats that response.

The [Phantom Blade Zero combat guide](/en/guides/combat-guide/) breaks down the parry/dodge rotation in more detail, but the practical takeaway is to train both reflexes before reaching Vermillion Gate. According to player reports, the average first-attempt clear rate is under 15% — this is the wall that separates regular players from the mastery crowd.

## Combat Fundamentals for Boss Fights

Bosses in Phantom Blade Zero aren't designed to be cheesed. They respond to specific player behaviors, and the skill ceiling is largely about reading tells rather than grinding levels. The fundamentals below carry through every encounter in this Phantom Blade Zero boss guide.

**Sha-Chi management** is the single most important resource to track. Every heavy attack, parry, and Breached punish costs Sha-Chi, which regenerates slowly. New players spend Sha-Chi on offense and run dry during the boss's recovery phase, which is when the boss's counterattack lands hardest. The [Phantom Blade Zero Sha-Chi guide](/en/guides/sha-chi-guide/) covers the exact regeneration rates and optimal spending patterns, but the rule of thumb is to spend no more than 60% of your Sha-Chi bar on offense during a boss fight.

**Dodge vs. parry** is the second decision point. Dodging has more i-frames but no counterattack follow-up, while parrying has fewer i-frames but staggers the boss. The [how to dodge tutorial](/en/guides/how-to-dodge/) explains that dodging leaves you vulnerable for an extra 0.2 seconds afterward, which is why many boss guides recommend parrying the first two hits of a combo and dodging the last. Mixed defense is the answer for every boss from Chapter 3 onward.

**Breached state** is the punish window that opens after you deplete the boss's posture bar — distinct from HP. Breached bosses can't attack for a brief duration, and every hit during that window deals bonus stagger damage. The trick is to save your heaviest Sha-Chi combo for the Breached state, not for the boss's regular recovery frames. The [how to parry tutorial](/en/guides/how-to-parry/) includes a specific training drill for building the muscle memory you'll need to consistently trigger Breached states.

**Stamina potions** should be reserved for the second phase. Most new players burn through their potions during the opening phase, which is the easiest part of every fight. Standard encounters in this Phantom Blade Zero boss guide generally require 4–6 healing items per attempt when played well, and skilled players can clear most bosses with 2–3.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the hardest boss in Phantom Blade Zero?**

The **Commander Cleave** fight at the end of Chapter 6 is widely considered the hardest because he counters any single defensive strategy, forcing you to alternate parry and dodge on a precise rotation.

**Can I skip the optional Red Wraith boss?**

Yes, but doing so reportedly alters the final battle's opening-phase buffs, and you'll miss out on a unique parry charm reward that makes the fight against Huangxing the Sunken noticeably easier.

**Should I use healing items or antidotes against Huangxing?**

Bring **antidotes** instead of basic healing potions for the Huangxing fight, because his flooded arena applies a poison DoT that regular healing items don't remove, while antidotes cleanse the effect instantly.

**When is the best time to use Sha-Chi combos?**

Save your heaviest Sha-Chi combo for the boss's Breached state, because hits during that window deal bonus stagger damage and extend the punish window — the same combo used on a non-Breached boss deals roughly 40% less total damage.

**What level should I be before attempting the late game bosses?**

Community testing suggests a baseline of level 45 with fully upgraded weapons for the Huangxing and Commander Cleave encounters, though skilled players can clear them earlier by relying on parry-dodge rotations rather than raw stats.
