## Act 1 Boss Encounters and Where to Find Them

Act 1 introduces the combat language of **Phantom Blade Zero** through two named fights and one hidden mini-boss that most beginners will walk past without realizing it qualifies as a boss encounter. None of these gates a build decision, so you can clear them with a balanced sword-and-shield setup, although experimenting with your parry timing here pays dividends later. New players who want a smoother first act should review the [Phantom Blade Zero beginner guide](/en/guides/beginner-guide/) alongside this list.

### Wan Jun Coppermaul — Warhammer Brute

The first true boss you meet is **Wan Jun Coppermaul**, a hulking militia captain stationed inside the Burning Granary courtyard. He swings a heavy warhammer that scatters loose dirt across the arena floor, which slows your dodge startup by roughly 0.2 seconds whenever you roll through a dirt patch. His combo is short — usually two overhead swings followed by a leaping stomp — but each hit deals chunky posture damage, so you cannot trade blows blindly.

| Attack | Tell | Punish Window | Recommended Response |
|--------|------|----------------|----------------------|
| Overhead Smash | Hilt glows red for 0.6s | 1.2s after impact | Dodge left, 2 light attacks |
| Leaping Stomp | Brief crouch then jump | 0.8s landing recovery | Backstep, then 1 heavy |
| Dirt Scatter (arena hazard) | Warhammer drags ground | Always active | Stay near edges, avoid center |

Coppermaul drops the **Granary Iron Ring**, which boosts stamina regeneration by 8 percent and is one of the best early accessories in the game.

### Red Wraith — Optional Rooftop Threat

**Red Wraith** appears only if you climb the unmarked watchtower in the eastern district of the Burning Granary hub. Skip him and the story continues normally, but killing him removes an enemy spawn from the final battle arena, which makes the closing fight noticeably easier on a first run. According to community testing, his move set mirrors the player's own sword combo, so if you have been button-mashing on trash mobs he will punish you hard.

If you want a smoother parry read, the [Phantom Blade Zero how to parry](/en/guides/how-to-parry/) breakdown walks through the timing windows that also apply here.

### Hollow Drumkeeper — Hidden Mini-Boss

Most players stumble into **Hollow Drumkeeper** by accident after breaking a brittle wall behind the millhouse. He is technically a mini-boss rather than a story boss, but he guards a chest containing the **Drumkeeper's Greaves**, which are a solid piece of armor through the mid-game.

## Act 2 Boss Encounters and Hidden Fights

Act 2 doubles the difficulty curve because every encounter here introduces either a new mechanic, a duo, or a transformation phase that punishes players who learned bad habits in Act 1. You will also encounter the game's signature seven-body formation fight, which is widely considered the most mechanically complex mandatory boss in **Phantom Blade Zero**.

### Chief Disciple of Seven Stars — Seven-Body Formation

The **Chief Disciple of Seven Stars** is a seven-enemy formation, not a single target. When you enter the moonlit training hall, six disciples spawn in a circle and the chief drops into the center once half of them are downed. The mechanic that catches most players off guard is the heal-sacrifice: any disciple still alive when the chief activates his second-phase chant will kneel and funnel health into him, refilling roughly 30 percent of his bar.

| Phase | Enemy Count | Priority Target | Risk if Ignored |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Phase 1 (entry) | 7 (chief + 6 disciples) | Outer disciples | Heal-sacrifice restores chief HP |
| Phase 2 (chant) | 1 (chief only) | Chief during chant cast | Unblockable AoE blast |
| Phase 3 (enraged) | 1 | Chief after 50% HP | Faster combo, new dash attack |

The cleanest clear is to burst down the outer disciples with area-of-effect combos, then burn the chief during his chant animation before he can absorb a sacrifice. Players who want to sharpen their dodging for the AoE blast can brush up with the [Phantom Blade Zero how to dodge](/en/guides/how-to-dodge/) guide.

### TieSha the Frenzy

**TieSha the Frenzy** is technically semi-optional because she appears only if you side with the Rebellion camp during the Act 2 crossroads decision. Choose the Imperial path and she replaces one of the regular arena fights with a scripted cutscene instead, which means you miss out on the **Rebellion War-Fan** weapon she drops. Her fighting style is relentless aggression with rapid twin-blade swings, and she can chain up to nine hits if you try to parry every strike.

### Bone-Tide Ronin — Optional Arena

**Bone-Tide Ronin** waits inside the flooded arena beneath the docks, accessible only after draining the water wheel during a side quest. He is slower than TieSha but hits far harder, and his tidal-themed attacks inflict the **Wet** status, which increases lightning damage taken by 25 percent.

## Late-Game Bosses and Optional Skirmishes

The third act is where **Phantom Blade Zero** stops holding your hand. Every encounter from here onward assumes you have a fully upgraded weapon, a working parry timing, and at least one pocket healing item. Optional bosses in this section also gate the alternate ending, so completionists cannot skip them without locking themselves out of a narrative branch.

### Huangxing the Sunken

**Huangxing the Sunken** is a corrupted water-wraith who guards the flooded temple you reach near the end of Act 3. His watery form splits into three phantom copies at 50 percent HP, and only the original takes posture damage; hitting a copy deals chip damage but resets your combo counter. According to community testing, the copies telegraph with a faint blue ripple 1.2 seconds before they spawn, so watch the floor, not the boss.

| Move | Tell | Counter |
|------|------|---------|
| Triple Split | Blue ripples on ground | Mark center figure, ignore copies |
| Tidal Slam | Water pools around his feet | Jump, punish landing |
| Drowning Pull (grab) | Arms extend forward | Dodge backward twice |

### Commander Cleave — Final Duel

**Commander Cleave** is the story's final boss and arguably the hardest encounter in the base game, because he cycles through every weapon type you have seen throughout the campaign. He opens with a warhammer phase, shifts to twin sabers at 60 percent HP, and finishes with a spear combo at 20 percent that inflicts the **Bleed** status on every successful hit. Builds that rely on a single weapon archetype get punished here, so consider a versatile loadout.

Players who cleared the **Red Wraith** earlier will notice his arena has fewer enemy spawns, which is the designer's quiet reward for thorough exploration. For broader preparation advice, the [Phantom Blade Zero tips and tricks](/en/guides/tips-and-tricks/) article covers how to assemble a flexible build for this final stretch.

### Verdant Crane Sentinel

**Verdant Crane Sentinel** waits at the mountaintop shrine that branches the alternate ending path. Skip her and you lock yourself onto the standard ending; defeat her and you unlock the **Crane's Oath** gesture plus a unique jade talisman that reduces posture damage taken by 12 percent.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many bosses are in Phantom Blade Zero?**

The base **Phantom Blade Zero all bosses list** includes ten confirmed encounters: four mandatory story bosses, two semi-optional fights with branching outcomes, and four hidden or optional bosses that reward exploration. Only four of them are required to roll the credits.

**Is the Red Wraith fight missable?**

Yes. The **Red Wraith** sits on the rooftop of the eastern watchtower in the Burning Granary hub, and there is no quest marker pointing to him. Skip him and the final battle against Commander Cleave spawns extra enemies, which makes the closing duel noticeably harder.

**What is the hardest boss in Phantom Blade Zero?**

Most players rank **Commander Cleave** as the hardest encounter because he cycles through three weapon phases, inflicts Bleed during his spear phase, and punishes players who built around a single weapon archetype. **Huangxing the Sunken** is a close second thanks to the copy mechanic that forces you to read floor telegraphs.

**Can you skip any boss fights?**

You can skip **Red Wraith**, **Bone-Tide Ronin**, **Verdant Crane Sentinel**, **Hollow Drumkeeper**, and **Salt-Worn General** without blocking the main ending, although skipping **Verdant Crane Sentinel** locks you out of the alternate ending branch. The four story bosses — Coppermaul, Seven Stars, Huangxing, and Cleave — cannot be skipped.

**What level should I be for late-game bosses?**

Community testing suggests level 24–26 for **Huangxing the Sunken** and level 28–30 for **Commander Cleave**, with a fully upgraded weapon of your preferred archetype. Going in under-geared is possible but tends to extend the fight to two or three attempts rather than one, because their posture bars scale with player level.
