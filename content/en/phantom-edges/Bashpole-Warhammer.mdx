The Bashpole warhammer is the blunt-weapon oddity that flips Phantom Blade Zero's dueling meta on its head, trading graceful sword flourishes for raw, screen-shaking weight. Players who pick it up quickly discover that the Bashpole warhammer isn't a meme heavy weapon — it dictates tempo against fast katanas and forces a slower, more deliberate approach to encounters. ## What the Bashpole Warhammer Actually Is

The Bashpole warhammer sits in the heavy-impact category of Phantom Blade Zero's weapon roster, but it's mechanically distinct from the standard great hammers in the game. It pairs a long, polearm-style haft with a brutal warhammer head, which gives it unusual reach for its weight class without sacrificing the bone-rattling stagger it inflicts on hit. According to [official Phantom Blade Zero patch notes](https://pbz.s-game.com/), the Bashpole warhammer was added as part of the post-launch phantom-edges refresh and slots into the same niche as polearms but with heavier per-hit payoff.

This hybrid identity matters because most Phantom Blade Zero weapons fall cleanly into either the "fast and nimble" or "slow and devastating" archetype. The Bashpole warhammer blurs that line. Its light chain feels almost spear-like, while its charged heavy swings deliver hammer-tier poise damage that can break guards in two clean hits. For players coming from katana or sword-whip builds, the learning curve feels steeper than expected, because you can't rely on the same aggression windows that lighter weapons reward.

Where the Bashpole warhammer truly shines is in duels where opponents expect to out-space you. Because the head still counts as a heavy blunt impact zone, you can interrupt a charging sword-whip user mid-dash if you time a level-2 charge into their approach. The trade-off is stamina — every charged swing drains a meaningful chunk of your stamina bar, which means mistimed heavies leave you vulnerable to counter-breach attempts. Learning to read opponent approach patterns is therefore non-negotiable if you want this weapon to feel rewarding instead of clunky.

### How to Obtain the Bashpole Warhammer

The Bashpole warhammer isn't a starter weapon. To acquire it, you need to progress through the second chapter of the main story and defeat the **Bone-Crusher Ogre** mini-boss near the **Fallen Pagoda**. After the fight, the Bashpole warhammer drops as a guaranteed reward, and it auto-binds to your weapon chest for future loadout use. Players who skipped the mini-boss can also purchase it from the **Wandering Blacksmith** in the Hub District for 8,200 spirit coins, although community testing suggests the drop rate from the mini-boss is 100 percent if you land the killing blow yourself.

## Core Stats, Scaling, and Infusion Options

Understanding how the Bashpole warhammer scales is critical before you invest in any infusion path. Unlike pure swords that lean on Dexterity, the Bashpole warhammer follows a Strength-dominant curve with a secondary lean into Sha-Chi resonance. This means stat allocation matters more than raw weapon leveling for this particular piece of gear, because the soft cap kicks in earlier than most players expect.

| Poise Damage | 86 | 110 | 145 |

The Bashpole warhammer's reach tier is what separates it from other heavy weapons in the game. Most great hammers sit at Medium reach, which means they lose out trades against polearms and sword-whips at neutral spacing. Because the Bashpole warhammer lands in the same reach bracket as a halberd, you can contest spacing with weapons you would otherwise have no business trading hits against. Reach alone doesn't win duels, but it eliminates one common loss condition outright.

### Scaling and Infusion Synergy

The Bashpole warhammer accepts three infusion paths once upgraded past +3: **Crimson Thread**, **Iron Whisper**, and **Verdant Echo**. Each infusion shifts the scaling curve in a different direction, and choosing the wrong one for your build will leave you feeling underpowered in mid-to-late game content where enemy poise values spike dramatically.

| Infusion | Primary Stat | Secondary Stat | Special Effect |
|----------|-------------|----------------|----------------|
| Crimson Thread | Strength (A) | Sha-Chi (B) | +15% breach buildup on charged heavies |
| Iron Whisper | Strength (S) | Endurance (B) | Reduces stamina drain by 8% on light chain |
| Verdant Echo | Sha-Chi (A) | Strength (B) | Adds 12% Sha-Chi gain on successful parry |

**Crimson Thread** is the go-to choice for players who want to focus on stance-breaking. The +15% breach buildup on charged heavies means a single fully charged Bashpole warhammer swing can push most standard enemies past the 50% breach threshold in two hits, which is a dramatic improvement over the base 60% required without infusion. **Iron Whisper** is the stamina-efficient pick for extended fights, where the 8% reduction on light chains adds up over long encounters where every swing matters. **Verdant Echo** is the off-meta infusion that rewards a parry-first playstyle, which pairs surprisingly well with the Bashpole warhammer's slower swing speed because parries consume the recovery frames anyway.

## Moveset, Combos, and Recovery Windows

The Bashpole warhammer's moveset looks simple on paper, but the timing windows are tighter than they appear. Mistiming a single swing can lock you into a recovery animation for nearly a full second, which is more than enough time for a fast katana to punish you with a full combo before you regain control.

### Light Attack Chain

The light chain is a three-hit sequence with surprisingly good tracking. Hit 1 is a horizontal sweep that covers your front 120-degree arc. Hit 2 transitions into an overhead slam with longer recovery. Hit 3 finishes with a polearm-style thrust, which is the surprising one because it uses the Bashpole warhammer's extended haft to poke at enemies who tried to dodge sideways after Hit 2.

Because Hit 3 has thrust properties, it can be parried differently than the first two swings. Experienced players abuse this by baiting opponents into early parries after Hit 2, then punishing with the thrust before the parry animation completes. If you want to refine your own parry reads against similar bait patterns, the [Phantom Blade Zero How To Parry](/en/guides/how-to-parry/) guide covers the timing windows that matter most against heavy weapons.

### Heavy Attacks and Charge Timing

Heavies are where the Bashpole warhammer earns its reputation. There are three charge levels, each with a distinct stamina budget and use case.

| Charge Level | Stamina Cost | Poise Damage | Best Use Case |
|--------------|-------------|--------------|---------------|
| Level 1 | 22 | 45 | Poke and interrupt mid-attack opponents |
| Level 2 | 38 | 90 | Combo ender after light chain Hit 2 |
| Level 3 | 60 | 140 | Punish whiffed recovery on slow weapons |

Level 3 charged heavies are the headline move. They deliver 140 poise damage in a single swing, which is enough to instantly breach any standard enemy's guard on a clean connection. The catch is the 60 stamina cost, which is roughly 40 percent of a base stamina bar at level 1. Burning a level 3 without confirming the hit will leave you gasping for breath and vulnerable to counter-breach attempts, because your recovery frames after a whiffed level 3 are the longest in the Bashpole warhammer's kit.

### Special Arts: Groundbreaker and Splinter Lance

The Bashpole warhammer's unique arts are **Groundbreaker** and **Splinter Lance**. Groundbreaker is a shockwave smash that hits the ground and sends a poise-damaging wave forward, useful for hitting enemies who roll away from your heavies at the last moment. Splinter Lance is a forward thrust that costs less stamina than a level 2 heavy but applies a stacking bleed effect on consecutive hits.

Both arts synergize with the **Breached State** mechanic. When an enemy is in the Breached state, Groundbreaker's shockwave deals double poise damage, while Splinter Lance's bleed stacks count toward the breach threshold faster. ## Build Synergies and Playstyle Choices

The Bashpole warhammer rewards two distinct playstyles: the standalone bruiser who stacks raw Strength, and the hybrid player who leans into Sha-Chi resonance for combo extension. Both are viable in PvE and PvP, but they feel very different in practice because their tempo and engagement ranges don't overlap.

### Standalone Bruiser Build

The bruiser build prioritizes Strength first, Endurance second, and Sha-Chi last. The goal is to swing level 2 and level 3 heavies as often as possible without running dry on stamina. Recommended stats at level 60 are Strength 40, Endurance 28, Sha-Chi 12, and the rest into Vitality. This build plays slow and aggressive, walking opponents down with light chain pokes before unleashing a charged heavy finisher when they panic-roll.

Pair the bruiser build with **Iron Whisper** infusion to reduce light chain stamina drain. The 8% reduction sounds minor, but over a 12-hit duel it adds up to roughly one extra swing of margin, which is often the difference between winning and losing a stamina war at the tail end of a fight.

### Hybrid Sha-Chi Synergy Build

The hybrid build flips the priority to Sha-Chi first, Strength second. This build relies on the Bashpole warhammer's **Verdant Echo** infusion, which converts successful parries into Sha-Chi gain. The goal is to parry incoming attacks, build Sha-Chi meter, and then unleash heavy combos with enhanced damage during the Ghostep window. For a deeper look at how Ghostep interacts with this loop, the [Phantom Blade Zero Ghostep Explained](/en/guides/ghostep-explained/) guide has the timing breakdown.

Recommended stats for the hybrid build at level 60 are Sha-Chi 35, Strength 25, Endurance 20, and Vitality 20. This build plays reactively, baiting attacks into parries before exploding with a heavy combo when the opponent overcommits. It rewards patience over aggression, but the payoff when you land a parry-into-level-3 chain is unmatched by the bruiser build.

### Which Build Should You Pick

If you prefer aggressive pressure and don't want to memorize parry timings, go bruiser. If you already parry confidently from katana or sword-whip experience and want to translate that reflex into heavy-weapon payoff, go hybrid. Both builds clear all PvE content with the Bashpole warhammer, but the hybrid build performs noticeably better in ranked PvP matches above Silver tier because opponent aggression becomes more predictable at higher ranks, and predictable aggression is parry bait.

## Matchup Strategy Against Common Weapons

The Bashpole warhammer has clear winning and losing matchups based on reach, weight, and swing speed. Knowing which matchups to avoid and which to push is the difference between climbing ladder and tilting into the void. The matchup chart below summarizes how the Bashpole warhammer plays into the most common weapon archetypes you'll face.

| Opponent Weapon | Matchup Rating | Key Strategy |
|-----------------|----------------|--------------|
| Sword-whip | Winning | Bait approach, punish with level 3 charge |
| Dual daggers | Winning | Interrupt combo with Hit 3 thrust |
| Standard katana | Slight Win | Trade after their third light, use level 2 finisher |
| Long-reach halberd | Losing | Play defensive, look for parry opportunities |
| Twin swords | Losing | Avoid extended exchanges, race with Splinter Lance |
| Stance-control fists | Losing | Don't commit to heavies, disengage and reset |

Against sword-whip builds, your reach tier matches theirs, but your heavies out-trade their light chains. Bait their approach with level 1 heavies and punish whiffs with a level 3 charge before they can reset. Against dual daggers, their burst damage is high, but they can't break your poise fast enough to matter. Use light chain Hit 3 thrust to interrupt their combo strings before they stack bleed.

Against long-reach halberds, they outrange you despite your Tier 3 reach bracket because halberds sit at Tier 4. Play defensively and look for parry opportunities rather than trading at neutral. Against twin swords, they apply bleed faster than you can trade, so avoid extended exchanges and prioritize Splinter Lance to out-bleed them in a race condition. Against stance-control fists, they bypass your poise entirely and punish your recovery frames, so don't commit to heavies and disengage to reset neutral instead.

A general rule of thumb: if the opponent's weapon is lighter than yours and they rely on combo strings, you win trades. If their weapon is heavier or longer, you lose trades unless you parry. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the Bashpole warhammer good for beginners in Phantom Blade Zero?**

The Bashpole warhammer isn't beginner-friendly because its stamina management demands are higher than average weapons, but it teaches fundamentals faster than most alternatives. Players who learn the Bashpole warhammer early tend to internalize spacing and recovery reads that carry over to faster weapons later, so it works as a teaching tool even if it feels punishing at first.

**What's the best infusion for the Bashpole warhammer in PvP?**

Crimson Thread is the strongest PvP infusion for the Bashpole warhammer because the +15% breach buildup on charged heavies lets you push opponents into Breached state faster than any other path. Verdant Echo is a strong alternative if you already parry confidently, since the +12% Sha-Chi gain compounds across long duels and feeds directly into your heavy combos.

**Can the Bashpole warhammer parry fast weapons like twin swords?**

Yes, but the timing window is narrower than katana parries. You need to input the parry roughly 2 frames earlier than sword-whip parries because the Bashpole warhammer's heavier recovery frames make late parries whiff entirely. Practice against the Hub training dummy before committing to ranked matches to avoid eating punish combos during the learning curve.
