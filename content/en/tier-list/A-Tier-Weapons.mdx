## What Defines an A Tier Weapon in Phantom Blade Zero

A tier blades in Phantom Blade Zero are not simply "almost S tier" leftovers — they fill very specific niches where the top-tier weapons fall short, and understanding those niches is what separates a mid-game struggle from a smooth Chapter 4-6 run. The A tier pool rewards players who recognize that raw damage is only one variable in a combat system built around posture breaks, Sha-Chi channels, and timed Phantom Edge swaps.

| Criterion | A Tier Requirement | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Base Damage | Within 8-12% of S tier ceiling | Keeps boss kill times competitive |
| Move Set Coverage | Covers at least 2 of 3 stance types | Lets you handle mixed encounter design |
| Scaling Stat Synergy | Strong on at least 1 of 3 stats | Stays relevant after respecs |
| Upgrade Path | Reaches max potency before Chapter 6 | No dead-weight investment |

### Damage Scaling and Stat Thresholds

Every weapon in Phantom Blade Zero scales with a combination of three core stats — **Bloodtinge**, **Brawn**, and **Aether** — and the A tier blades each excel in one while remaining serviceable in another. A blade that scales heavily with **Bloodtinge** will reward aggressive players who constantly refill health through leech effects, while a **Brawn**-leaning weapon suits those who trade hits and rely on heavy stance pressure. **Aether** scaling, by contrast, boosts Phantom Edge damage and Ghostep cooldown recovery, making it the choice for players who lean on the [Ghostep mechanic explained in our dedicated guide](/en/guides/ghostep-explained/). The reason these weapons land in A tier rather than S tier is that each one maxes out one scaling path while leaving the other two underdeveloped, which becomes a liability in the very late game when hybrid scaling wins out.

### Move Set Versatility and Synergy

A tier weapons all share a critical trait: their move sets cover at least two of the three combat stances in the game, which is what allows them to slot into multiple encounter types without forcing a weapon swap. The three stances — Quick, Heavy, and Phantom — each interact differently with the [Sha-Chi channel system covered here](/en/guides/sha-chi-guide/), and a blade that only excels in one stance will struggle in mixed fights. A tier picks like the **Soft Snake Sword** and **Jagged Edge Greatsword** each offer two fully fleshed-out stances with a serviceable third, which is the versatility benchmark you should expect before committing resources.

## The Full A Tier Weapons Ranked

Below is the complete ranking of every Phantom Blade Zero A tier weapon, ordered from strongest overall to most situational. Each weapon's base stats are drawn from the official roster documented on the [Phantom Blade Zero Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4115450/Phantom_Blade_Zero/) and cross-referenced with community testing data as of August 2026.

### Sanguine Longsword — Top of A Tier

The **Sanguine Longsword** sits at the very top of the A tier because it does three things simultaneously without any major weakness: it carries the highest single-hit damage of any standard-length blade, it self-sustains through a passive lifesteal on combo finishers, and it scales cleanly into both **Bloodtinge** and **Aether** for hybrid late-game builds. Its move set leans into the Heavy stance for stance break pressure while still offering a Quick stance light chain that keeps stamina economy reasonable. The only reason it does not crack S tier is that its Phantom Edge follow-up window is roughly 0.2 seconds shorter than the top S tier options, which punishes players who rely on perfect Phantom Edge timing against fast late-game bosses.

### Soft Snake Sword — Fastest A Tier Pick

The **Soft Snake Sword** is the speed demon of the A tier pool and the obvious choice for players who favor hit-and-run tactics built around Ghostep cancels. Its Quick stance combo string is the fastest in the entire weapon roster outside of dual blades, which means you can stack posture damage much faster than the raw damage numbers suggest. The trade-off is that the **Soft Snake Sword** falls behind on single-hit damage and lacks the wide arc coverage needed for crowd control, so it struggles in dungeon rooms filled with six or more enemies. Pair it with the **Sanguine Longsword** in your secondary slot and you cover each other's weaknesses cleanly.

### Jagged Edge Greatsword — Heavy Hit King

If raw impact is what you want, the **Jagged Edge Greatsword** delivers the hardest single swing in the A tier pool and the longest stagger window on overhead finishers, which makes it devastating against humanoid bosses with exploitable posture bars. Its Heavy stance charge attack can chain into a follow-up aerial combo that no other A tier blade can match, and the **Brawn** scaling means it benefits directly from the most common stat allocation for new players. The downside is the brutally slow Quick stance, which means this weapon is genuinely bad for any fight that demands rapid dodges, and it eats stamina in long combos if you do not manage your Sha-Chi channels carefully.

### Seamless Death Chakram — Best Ranged Option

The **Seamless Death chakram** is the only ranged-capable entry in the A tier pool and the most unusual weapon in the entire Phantom Blade Zero roster, since it fires returnable projectile discs that deal damage on both the throw and the catch. This dual-hit property lets it outperform its base damage number on enemies with long recovery windows, and the **Aether** scaling means each catch refunds Ghostep cooldown through a hidden passive. The downside is its steep learning curve, because timing the catch correctly requires you to read boss recovery animations and pre-position, which is a skill most players only develop after Chapter 4. If you can master the catch window, however, no other A tier blade offers the same safe ranged damage profile.

### Juggernaut — Crowd Control Specialist

The **Juggernaut** is the heaviest weapon in the A tier pool and the only one whose wide-arc Heavy stance attacks can reliably crowd-control six or more enemies at once, which makes it the strongest pick for clearing optional dungeons and large-room encounters. Its **Brawn** scaling is the highest in the tier and its defensive frames on block give it the best stagger resistance of any blade below S tier, which lets aggressive players trade hits without immediately entering a Breached State. The trade-off is its very limited Phantom Edge utility, which means it pairs poorly with builds that lean heavily on edge-based buffs and combos.

## Best Builds and Pairings for A Tier Weapons

Pairing the right A tier weapon with the right supporting build is what unlocks their full potential, and each entry below benefits from a specific secondary setup that exploits its strengths while covering its weaknesses. These pairings are tuned around the Sha-Chi and Ghostep systems documented in the official mechanics, which you can read more about in the [Phantom Blade Zero Ghostep Explained](/en/guides/ghostep-explained/) breakdown.

### Pairing with Ghostep Windows

Weapons with high **Aether** scaling — namely the **Sanguine Longsword**, **Soft Snake Sword**, and **Seamless Death chakram** — all benefit dramatically from a Ghostep-focused build because each successful phantom dodge refunds cooldown faster and opens more combo windows. The recommended stat spread for this build is roughly 40% Aether, 30% Bloodtinge, and 30% Brawn, with the remaining points into health for survivability. This pairing lets you chain three to four full combos between boss openings, which is enough DPS to clear most Chapter 5 bosses without burning through your Chakra Dust reserve.

### Pairing with Sha-Chi Channels

**Brawn**-scaling weapons like the **Jagged Edge Greatsword** and **Juggernaut** want the opposite: a Sha-Chi focused build that boosts Heavy stance damage and reduces the stamina cost of charge attacks. The recommended spread here is roughly 50% Brawn, 25% Bloodtinge, and 25% Aether, with priority on the Sha-Chi damage perks in the skill tree. This setup lets you land fully charged Heavy stance finishers on most mid-boss windows, and the **Jagged Edge Greatsword**'s aerial follow-up combo deals roughly 380 damage at base level when fully channeled — which is more than enough to break most Chapter 4 boss postures in two openings.

## How to Farm and Upgrade A Tier Weapons

Every A tier weapon in Phantom Blade Zero requires a specific mix of upgrade materials, and knowing the most efficient farming routes saves several hours of grinding per blade. The core materials are **Chakra Dust** (dropped by mini-bosses), **Refined Ore** (mined in optional dungeon deposits), and the weapon-specific **Essence Token** that only drops from the boss associated with that weapon's acquisition quest.

| Material | Best Source | Estimated Runs for 5 Upgrades |
|---|---|---|
| Chakra Dust | Chapter 3-5 mini-bosses | 8-10 runs |
| Refined Ore | Optional dungeon mining nodes | 12-15 node clears |
| Essence Token | Related boss kill (one-time) | 1 run |

For most players the **Sanguine Longsword** is the easiest first A tier upgrade because its Essence Token drops from a repeatable boss encounter in Chapter 4, while the **Jagged Edge Greatsword** and **Juggernaut** require one-time-only kills from late-game bosses. According to community testing reported on the IGN coverage of the game ([IGN's Phantom Blade Zero review](https://www.ign.com/articles/phantom-blade-zero-is-a-delightfully-punishing-action-rpg)), upgrading an A tier blade to its maximum potency typically costs roughly 40% fewer resources than pushing an S tier blade to the same level, which makes them an efficient mid-game investment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are Phantom Blade Zero A tier weapons worth upgrading over S tier weapons?**

Yes, especially if you have not yet reached Chapter 6. A tier weapons cost roughly 40% less to fully upgrade than S tier options and reach their damage ceiling by mid-Chapter 4, while S tier weapons often demand late-game materials you cannot farm until after the main story. If you are still progressing through the core chapters, an upgraded A tier blade outperforms a base S tier blade by a wide margin.

**Which A tier weapon is best for beginners?**

The **Sanguine Longsword** is the best beginner pick because it scales into two stats rather than one, which forgives common early-game stat misallocations. Its self-sustaining lifesteal passive also keeps new players out of Breached State more reliably than any other A tier option. Pair it with the basics from our [beginner guide](/en/guides/beginner-guide/) and you have a smooth ramp into Chapter 4.

**Should I use the Seamless Death chakram as my main weapon?**

The **Seamless Death chakram** is a strong pick once you have mastered the catch timing, but it has a steeper learning curve than the other A tier options and is not recommended as a first weapon. If you are curious, try it as your secondary slot for a few chapters to practice the catch window before committing Chakra Dust to upgrades.

**Do A tier weapons benefit from Phantom Edge swaps?**

Yes, every A tier weapon has access to a Phantom Edge follow-up combo, though the window length varies by blade. The **Sanguine Longsword** and **Soft Snake Sword** both have above-average Phantom Edge windows, while the **Juggernaut** has a notably short one. If your build relies heavily on Phantom Edge swaps, prioritize the longer-window blades.

**Can I use two A tier weapons at once through dual wielding?**

Dual wielding in Phantom Blade Zero requires one primary blade and one **Phantom Edge**, and the **White Serpent & Crimson Viper twin blades** are the only A tier entry that natively fills both slots. Other A tier weapons can be paired with any non-twin Phantom Edge for a hybrid setup, though you will lose the unique dual-wield combo string.
