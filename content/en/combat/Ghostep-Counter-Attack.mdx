The Ghostep counter attack sits at the very top of the skill ceiling in Phantom Blade Zero, because it converts a single defensive read into a full punish chain that no other response can match. According to the official description on [Phantom Blade Zero on Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4115450/Phantom_Blade_Zero/), Ghostep is a repositioning counter triggered by a perfect parry on a brutal move or a perfect dodge on a killer move, after which Soul teleports behind the enemy to unleash a devastating counterattack. Players who learn when each trigger fires, and how to chain the follow-ups, turn ordinary fights into no-damage clears.

## What Is the Ghostep Counter Attack

The Ghostep counter attack is a **high-skill reposition mechanic** that rewards precise defensive reads on the two strongest enemy move categories. Rather than simply blocking or rolling away, Soul performs a short-range teleport behind the enemy, which skips their post-attack recovery frames and opens a guaranteed punish window. Because the reposition is automated by the game, the skill expression lives entirely in the read you make beforehand and the inputs you chain afterwards.

For a broader look at how Ghostep fits into the overall combat loop, the [Phantom Blade Zero Combat Guide](/en/guides/combat-guide/) covers the surrounding parry, dodge, and Breached-state systems, while this article focuses specifically on triggering and chaining the Ghostep counter attack. The [Phantom Blade Zero Official Site](https://pbz.s-game.com/) describes Ghostep as the core high-skill mechanic, which is why almost every late-game boss fight is tuned around it.

| Move Category | Trigger Input | Ghostep Result | Punish Window |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brutal Move** | **Perfect Parry** | Teleport behind enemy | 8–12 frames |
| **Killer Move** | **Perfect Dodge** | Teleport behind enemy | 6–10 frames |
| **Standard Move** | Block or normal dodge | No Ghostep | — |
| **Unparryable Move** | Cannot parry | No Ghostep | — |

The two trigger paths produce slightly different punish windows, because killer moves carry more hitstop than brutal moves, which is why the rest of this guide treats them as separate muscle-memory skills rather than a single reflex. Both routes still feed the same follow-up system, so building competence in one automatically raises your ceiling on the other.

### Why Ghostep Defines Late-Game Combat

Most bosses in Phantom Blade Zero enter a Breached state after enough stagger damage, and Ghostep is the only response that converts a Breached-state trigger into a full combo without leaving you exposed. Because Soul teleports behind the target, the enemy cannot retaliate with their usual punish rotation, which makes Ghostep the safest aggressive option in your toolkit if you can land it consistently. Once you learn the read, you stop fearing brutal and killer moves and start baiting them.

## Brutal Move Triggers and Perfect Parry

Brutal moves are the slower, heavily telegraphed attacks that an enemy charges up with a glowing weapon or a distinct windup animation. Because they are designed to be parried, they give you the largest Ghostep punish window once you land a **perfect parry**, which means the block must connect within the final few frames of the windup rather than at any earlier moment. According to community testing, brutal moves appear roughly once every four or five attack cycles in a typical boss pattern, which gives you predictable windows to prepare.

### Timing Window for Perfect Parry

The perfect parry window for a brutal move opens at roughly the last 6 frames of the windup and closes the instant the strike begins moving forward. Missing the window on either side drops you back into a regular parry, which still staggers the enemy but does **not** trigger the Ghostep counter attack. Practicing the windup cadence against a low-tier enemy is faster than trying to learn it during a boss, because brutal move tells are shorter on easier fights and let you reset quickly.

### Visual and Audio Cues

Brutal moves announce themselves with three consistent signals that you can train yourself to recognize without guessing:

- **Weapon glow**: the enemy's blade, fist, or pole arm emits a **bright gold or red tint** during the windup, which only appears on parryable heavy moves and never on standard strikes
- **Audio stinger**: a low brass swell or percussive thunk plays exactly when the windup begins, giving you an auditory frame-zero if you cannot watch the screen directly
- **Stance shift**: the enemy drops their weight slightly before the strike, which is most visible on humanoid bosses because their shoulders rotate backwards before the swing

If you are still building baseline parry consistency, the [Phantom Blade Zero How To Parry](/en/guides/how-to-parry/) guide covers the universal parry timing before you layer brutal-move reads on top. Once you can hold the trigger early enough to react to the glow or audio, the brutal-move Ghostep counter attack becomes a repeatable source of stagger damage.

## Killer Move Triggers and Perfect Dodge

Killer moves are unparryable attacks designed to punish players who hold the parry button out of habit, because they ignore block frames entirely and connect regardless of guard state. The Ghostep counter attack still works on killer moves, but the trigger flips from parry to a **perfect dodge**, which is a dodge that begins within a tight i-frame window matched to the incoming strike. Because killer moves have longer hitstop than brutal moves, landing the Ghostep counter attack on one feels heavier and opens a slightly different punish chain than the parry route.

### Timing Window for Perfect Dodge

The perfect dodge window on a killer move spans roughly the 10 frames where Soul would otherwise be hit, and the game confirms the read with a brief blue flash on Soul's silhouette when the i-frames line up. A normal dodge, even one timed well, only grants standard i-frames and will **not** trigger the Ghostep counter attack, which is why killer moves are the most common cause of failed Ghostep attempts among intermediate players. If you are still struggling with dodge consistency, the [Phantom Blade Zero How To Dodge](/en/guides/how-to-dodge/) article walks through the i-frame ranges before tackling killer-move reads.

### Ghostep Positioning After a Killer Move

Unlike a brutal-move Ghostep, which lands you close to the enemy's original facing, a killer-move Ghostep repositions you directly behind the target at mid-range, which is ideal for launching a chain of mid-priority attacks rather than a single heavy strike. This is why most advanced players reserve their heavy combos for parry-triggered Ghosteps and use dodge-triggered Ghosteps to extend into aerial or running follow-ups. The reposition is automatic, so your only job is to read the killer move in advance and commit to the dodge before the strike lands.

## Combo Chains After a Successful Ghostep

Once the Ghostep counter attack fires, the game grants you an extended punish window during which enemy stagger cannot end early and you can chain multiple attacks without risk of interruption. The exact length depends on the move that triggered the Ghostep, the enemy's Breached state, and your current weapon, but the pattern below is what experienced players target in each scenario. Because the teleport removes the enemy from their defensive stance, every chain you build during the punish window effectively becomes free damage.

### Punish Window Length by Trigger

| Trigger Source | Approximate Punish Window | Best Follow-up Type |
|---|---|---|
| **Brutal Move Parry** | **8–12 frames** | Heavy single strike |
| **Killer Move Dodge** | **6–10 frames** | Mid chain into aerial |
| **Breached Boss** | **12–16 frames** | Full combo finisher |
| **Standard Enemy** | **4–6 frames** | Light chain only |

### Recommended Follow-up Inputs

For brutal-move Ghosteps against a non-Breached enemy, a single **charged heavy attack** from your longest-reaching weapon maximizes the damage you can land inside the 8–12 frame window. For killer-move Ghosteps, a two-hit **mid chain into a launcher** works better, because the longer hitstop from the killer move gives you room to confirm the launch before the enemy recovers. ## Advanced Ghostep Strategy and Timing Windows

Beyond single trigger reads, the **ghostep counter attack** becomes truly dominant when you stack multiple reads in a single fight, because each successful Ghostep advances the stagger meter and pushes the enemy closer to a Breached state. At higher difficulties, bosses occasionally chain a brutal move into a killer move within the same recovery window, which is a deliberate pattern that rewards players who can swap from parry to dodge without thinking. According to community testing on the hardest encounters, the average successful Ghostep rate among veteran players sits around 60–70% of available triggers, which is enough to keep stagger pressure continuous across long fights.

### Boss-Specific Tactics

Most late-game bosses telegraph one brutal move and one killer move in their standard rotation, which means a single encounter is rarely about one trigger but about reading the full rotation and committing to the correct input each cycle. Against humanoid bosses, the brutal move almost always arrives on the third attack in the chain, which lets you plan a parry two strikes ahead. Against beast-type bosses, the killer move usually follows a charge, which means you pre-dodge instead of holding parry. General tips from the [Phantom Blade Zero Tips And Tricks](/en/guides/tips-and-tricks/) page also help, because small stamina and chip-damage habits compound into enough leftover health to survive a missed read.

### Reading Multi-Trigger Fights

In fights where a boss mixes brutal and killer moves, the safe rhythm is to parry the first brutal trigger, dodge the next killer trigger, then return to parry for the third cycle, which keeps your defensive posture consistent and avoids the most common mistake of holding parry through a killer move. Because the Ghostep counter attack animation locks Soul briefly after the teleport, spacing the triggers by at least one full enemy attack cycle prevents you from being caught in recovery when the next read window opens. Once you can sustain this rhythm across an entire phase, the boss becomes a stagger-race that the Ghostep counter attack reliably wins.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the Ghostep counter attack in Phantom Blade Zero?**

The Ghostep counter attack is a high-skill reposition mechanic where Soul teleports behind an enemy after a perfect parry on a brutal move or a perfect dodge on a killer move, opening a guaranteed punish window that lets you chain free damage while the enemy is still in recovery.

**Why does my Ghostep counter attack not trigger sometimes?**

The Ghostep counter attack only fires on perfect inputs, so a regular parry, a mistimed parry, or a standard dodge will all negate the trigger even if the defensive move itself succeeds. Check that you are reading brutal versus killer moves correctly, because trying to parry a killer move is the most common reason the Ghostep counter attack fails to activate.

**Can I chain multiple Ghosteps in one fight?**

Yes, chaining Ghosteps is the intended way to push bosses into Breached state, and the Ghostep counter attack cooldown is short enough that you can trigger it on every available brutal or killer move window provided you read each one correctly. Builds focused on stagger or Sha-Chi uptime extend this further by keeping enemies in vulnerable states longer between triggers.

**Does the Ghostep counter attack work on every boss?**

The Ghostep counter attack works on every boss that has at least one brutal or killer move in their pattern, which covers all main-story bosses and most elite encounters. Standard fodder enemies rarely include brutal moves, so Ghostep is primarily a boss-fight mechanic rather than a trash-clear tool.

**How does Ghostep differ from the standard parry punish?**

A standard parry stagger lets you land one or two follow-up attacks before the enemy recovers, whereas the Ghostep counter attack teleports you behind the target, skips their recovery frames, and opens a longer punish window that supports full combo chains and stagger extension during Breached state.
