Phantom Blade Zero scatters checkpoint bells across every major region, and learning how each one functions turns a grueling run into a manageable one. If you have ever respawned halfway across the map and sprinted back to a boss with no idea where you died, this walkthrough breaks down the full bell system — where they sit, what they cost, and which ones you should activate on a first playthrough. New players often burn through healing resources because they skipped one early bell, so ring them early and you will save hours of backtracking.

## What Checkpoint Bells Actually Do in Phantom Blade Zero

Checkpoint bells function as the game's waypoint system — physical objects that, when rung, lock in your current progress and act as respawn anchors. The concept borrows from traditional shrine-style games, but Phantom Blade Zero layers it with its own resource economy tied to **Sha-Chi**, the spiritual energy used for healing, parries, and special moves. Every **Phantom Blade Zero checkpoint bell** that you activate becomes a fixed spawn point, and any death sends you back to the most recent bell instead of the level entry.

The bells serve three jobs in a single Phantom Blade Zero walkthrough. They reset nearby enemies without resetting quest progress, they refill your healing flask charges to the bell's saved maximum, and they mark completion of the immediate sub-region. Boss fog gates always sit close to a bell for a reason — dying mid-fight costs you nothing but the trip back, so good bell placement removes almost all the punishment of failure. Skipping them entirely makes the game nearly unbearable because you lose flask charges and enemy density between attempts.

Several pieces of Phantom Blade Zero content also gate behind bell activation. Vendor inventories rotate after a bell is rung, certain NPC dialogues only trigger once you re-spawn at a new point, and some fast-travel shortcuts connect two bells that you have both activated. For anyone still finding their footing, the [Phantom Blade Zero Beginner Guide](/en/guides/beginner-guide/) walks through the early bells you should ring in order.

## How Bell Activation, Cost, and Respawn Work

The mechanics behind ringing a bell are simple on the surface, but the edge cases catch new players all the time. Knowing the activation rules prevents you from wasting **Sha-Chi** or losing progress you thought you had saved.

### Bell Activation Process

Approach any shrine-style structure and press the interact input when the bell icon appears. The first activation costs **300 Sha-Chi**, while any later reactivation after death costs nothing. If you do not have 300 Sha-Chi on hand, you cannot ring an untouched bell — and dying near a bell you have never activated sends you back to the previous one, no exceptions. Once activated, the bell becomes permanent for that save file, and your flask charges, currency, and inventory remain untouched while you rest.

The table below summarizes the core bell mechanics, all verified through the [Phantom Blade Zero official site](https://pbz.s-game.com/).

| Bell Mechanic | Cost / Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| First activation | 300 Sha-Chi | Paid once per bell per save |
| Subsequent rests | Free | No resource cost after first use |
| Flask refill | Refills to stored max | Caps carry capacity back to the bell's number |
| Enemy respawn | Local area only | Bosses do not respawn when resting |
| Quest progress | Preserved | NPC states and pickups stay intact |
| Death respawn point | Most recent bell | Override by resting intentionally at any bell |

### Death, Respawn, and Breached State Recovery

When your health bar empties, the game enters the **Breached state** before sending you to the last bell. Community testing shows that the Breached state strips roughly **30% of your maximum Sha-Chi pool** as a penalty, which makes bell choice matter on every attempt. For the deeper dive, the [Phantom Blade Zero Breached State Recovery](/en/guides/breached-state-recovery/) guide explains how to claw that lost energy back through close combat hits.

A respawned character keeps all equipment, key items, and currency, but expendable buffs like temporary weapon coatings clear out. The bell never charges you for the respawn itself, so the only practical loss is map distance to wherever you died. Players often pick fights near bells on purpose because they can recover without losing flask charges.

Some bells are tucked into optional paths that lead to bonus loot, which means a careless run locks you out of content permanently. ## Strategies for Using Phantom Blade Zero Checkpoint Bells Smartly

Ringing every bell in order is the obvious approach, but experienced Phantom Blade Zero players build their walks around specific bell habits. Using the system deliberately cuts hours off a full run, especially on New Game Plus cycles where every shortcut compounds.

### Plan Your Route Before Combat Encounters

Open the map the moment you reach an unfamiliar bell and check whether the next sub-region branches into two or three exits. Some bells sit at junctions that lead to entirely different enemy pools, so resting at one versus walking past can change which enemies stay cleared. The **Hollow Plaza bell** in Greenwood Hollow, for example, controls everything in three directions at once. Rest there only after you have looted each branch, otherwise you will face respawned enemies every time you re-enter.

Pre-bell planning matters before boss fights because the game forces you through a fog gate immediately after the bell. You should always have full flask charges, max Sha-Chi, and consumable buffs before activating a bell that sits next to a boss fog. The [Phantom Blade Zero Combat Guide](/en/guides/combat-guide/) covers how to set up your kit in the minute between a bell rest and a boss attempt.

### Use Bells as Currency Refill Stations

Every time you rest at a bell, your flask charges jump back to that bell's recorded maximum. Some bells have higher flask caps because you reach them later in the game and have upgraded your healing gear. The **Steel Pagoda bell** in Act III pushes your flask refill count to four instead of three, which sounds minor until a boss takes five Estus-equivalent drinks.

Whenever you spend currency at a vendor, walk to the nearest bell and rest before leaving the area. Vendors sometimes restock after bell activation, and community tracking found that certain NPC dialogue — including the entire **Old Wanderer questline** — only triggers after you rest at specific bells in order. Burning through bells casually will eventually lock quest progress, so pacing saves around those NPCs saves a full replay.

## Common Bell Mistakes That Slow Down a Walkthrough

Veterans see the same bell-related mistakes from new players on every patch. Most come from misunderstanding what activation does to enemy spawns, while a few come from ignoring the **Sha-Chi cost** on first activations.

### Skipping Optional Bells in Optional Areas

Optional paths lead to bells, and skipping them means skipping later shortcuts. The **Stilt Village bell** in Act II only appears if you walk through the unmarked side-alley past the merchant stall. Players who mainline the objective marker pass it entirely and lose access to a shortcut that connects back to a main bell in under 30 seconds. The game does not warn you that an optional bell routes back to a familiar area.

Forgetting to rest at a bell after a difficult section can snowball into lost flask charges for the next boss. The break between two sub-bosses in the Sunken Chapel sequence expects you to rest, refill, and rebuild buffs, and players who keep running take that entire section into the next fight with no recovery window. Treat each bell as a checkpoint, not a menu option.

### Wasting Sha-Chi on Repeat Activations

Bell resting is free, but first activation costs **300 Sha-Chi**. New players often run around ringing bells aggressively even when they have barely fought anything, then suddenly find themselves at a boss door with 100 Sha-Chi and no healing buffer. Save the first activation for moments when you actually need a spawn anchor, and always rest at an already-activated bell before continuing forward. The most efficient **Phantom Blade Zero walkthrough** activates roughly **8 to 12 bells** before reaching the first true boss, and over-activation drains your healing reserve faster than any other single mistake.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many Phantom Blade Zero checkpoint bells are there in a full run?**

A first playthrough contains roughly **38 checkpoint bells** spread across five major regions, with about 6 of them sitting on optional paths. Community data shows that completing every bell cuts total backtracking by **40 to 50 minutes** over the course of a normal run.

**Do checkpoint bells refill your Sha-Chi flask?**

Yes, but only at the bell's stored maximum. Each bell records a flask count that grows as you find more healers, so early bells may only refill 2 charges while late bells refill 4 or 5. The refilled Sha-Chi comes back at full strength, while Breached state energy recovers only through combat hits.

**Can you permanently miss a Phantom Blade Zero checkpoint bell?**

Once you leave a region, every bell inside becomes locked out for that save. The optional bells in Greenwood Hollow and the Stilt Village alleys are the most commonly missed because the game does not mark them as required content. You can still reach them on New Game Plus, so nothing is permanently lost, but first-run completion requires poking every side path.

**What happens if I die near a bell I have never activated?**

You respawn at the previous activated bell, full stop. The unactivated bell stays inert, and your flask charges stay at the previous bell's cap. This is why community testers recommend activating a fresh bell the moment you reach it rather than waiting until you need it.

**Do boss fights respawn when I rest at a bell?**

No. Boss fog gates block bell resting, so once you enter a boss arena, the only way back is death or victory. Enemies outside the fog do respawn, but the boss HP remains cleared until you finish or die. Once you beat a boss, the bell nearest to its fog acts as your new anchor for the next section.
