## How to Dodge in Phantom Blade Zero: Killer Moves, I-Frames & Recovery

Dodging is the heartbeat of combat in Phantom Blade Zero. Master it, and you'll slice through the game's most dangerous enemies with fluid precision. Get it wrong, and a single misread ends Soul's 66-day countdown. This guide breaks down every layer of the dodge system — from recognizing attack types to pulling off the perfect Ghostep counter.

Before diving in, confirm your understanding of the core defensive triangle: Parry, Dodge, and Block. Each has a specific role, and confusing them leads to unnecessary damage. For a broader overview of all three, check our guide on the /en/guides/combat-guide/ first.

### Recognizing Killer Moves vs Brutal Moves

The game's combat design gives you a clear visual cue before every enemy attack lands. **Killer Moves** are marked by a **red indicator** on the enemy's attack animation. These are lethal strikes that must be **dodged**, not parried. Attempting to parry a killer move will break your guard and leave Soul vulnerable to a devastating follow-up.

**Brutal Moves**, by contrast, flash **gold or orange** and are designed to be **parried**. These attacks are powerful but fair — they reward precise timing with a guard break opportunity and a chance to counter.

This color-coded system forms the foundation of Phantom Blade Zero's decision tree. If you see red, dodge. If you see gold, parry. The distinction is critical because the game's entire high-level combat loop depends on reading these cues correctly. The Steam page at https://store.steampowered.com/app/4115450/Phantom_Blade_Zero/ highlights the game's emphasis on this "攻防一体" (offense and defense as one) philosophy, and the dodge system is where that philosophy becomes most tangible.

### Why You Must Dodge Killer Moves (Never Parry Them)

Killer Moves carry significantly higher damage than Brutal Moves and often come with unblockable properties or multi-hit sequences. Parrying a killer move does not trigger the guard-break reward you get from parrying a Brutal Move — instead, it results in a **guard break** and a lengthy **recovery animation** that leaves Soul open to a combo.

The correct response is always a full dodge. The i-frame window on a well-timed dodge is your only reliable way to avoid killer move damage entirely. Some enemies chain Brutal and Killer moves together, so misidentifying the second hit as a Brutal Move and attempting to parry it is one of the most common mistakes new players make.

### I-Frame Timing and Dodge Windows

Phantom Blade Zero's dodge grants a brief period of **invincibility frames (i-frames)** during the initial dodge animation. The exact duration is short — roughly comparable to the dodge window in Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice — but the game provides generous visual telegraphing on enemy attacks to compensate.

The i-frame window activates at the **start** of the dodge roll, not during the recovery. This means your timing window is tight but fair: dodge too early and you'll still be vulnerable during the roll; dodge too late and the attack lands. The sweet spot is just as the red indicator reaches its peak or the enemy's strike begins its wind-up.

For players struggling with timing, practicing against lower-tier enemies first is recommended. The game's difficulty scales progressively, and early zones introduce Killer Moves with longer telegraphs, giving you room to build muscle memory before facing the faster enemies in later areas.

### Dodge Recovery Animations and Punishment Windows

Every dodge in Phantom Blade Zero ends with a **recovery animation**. During this recovery, Soul is vulnerable and cannot act. Understanding how long each recovery lasts is essential for knowing when you can safely attack or when you need to dodge again.

The recovery duration varies slightly depending on the weapon you're wielding and whether you dodged forward, backward, or sideways. Light weapons generally recover faster than heavy Juggernaut-class weapons, which aligns with the game's broader risk-reward weapon design philosophy.

The key strategic insight is that **dodge-canceling attacks** can interrupt the recovery animation. By inputting an attack during the final frames of recovery, you can skip the vulnerable window and begin your next offensive immediately. This technique is what separates casual players from those who can maintain constant pressure.

### Dodge-Canceling Attacks

Dodge-canceling is one of the most important advanced techniques in Phantom Blade Zero. After a successful dodge, you can immediately input an attack button to cancel the recovery animation and begin a combo. This works with most weapons, including the default Sanguine and Phantom Edges like White Serpent and Crimson Viper.

The cancel window is tight, so practice is required. Start by dodging toward an enemy and immediately pressing the attack button as soon as the dodge animation ends. With correct timing, Soul will transition seamlessly from the dodge into a full combo string without any pause.

This technique is especially powerful against enemies that throw multiple Brutal Moves in sequence. By dodge-canceling into an attack between their combos, you can deal damage while staying safe — effectively turning their aggression into your opportunity.

### The Decision Tree: Dodge vs Parry

The complete decision tree for Phantom Blade Zero's defense looks like this:

1. **Red indicator (Killer Move)** → Dodge immediately. Do not parry.
2. **Gold/orange indicator (Brutal Move)** → Parry at the correct timing window.
3. **No indicator / rapid multi-hit** → Default to dodge, as the risk of misreading is too high.
4. **Perfect parry of Brutal Move** → Counter opportunity and Sha-Chi gain.
5. **Perfect dodge of Killer Move** → Potential Ghostep trigger if conditions are met.

Following this tree consistently will dramatically reduce the damage you take and maximize your counter opportunities. Remember that Sha-Chi management plays a role here too — if you're low on Sha-Chi and your guard is Breached, you cannot parry Brutal Moves at all, making dodge the only safe option.

### Triggering Ghostep Through Extreme Dodging

The most rewarding reason to master the dodge system is accessing **Ghostep**, the game's signature counter maneuver. Ghostep activates when you **perfectly dodge a Killer Move** or **perfectly parry a Brutal Move** at the very edge of the attack's impact.

When Ghostep triggers, Soul teleports behind the enemy and delivers a devastating counterattack. This move is not just visually spectacular — it deals massive damage and can interrupt enemy combos entirely. The Ghostep mechanic rewards players who commit fully to the dodge timing rather than playing defensively.

To practice Ghostep, find an enemy that uses Killer Moves with clear telegraphs. Focus on dodging at the absolute last possible frame. When you see the flash and feel the teleport, you've nailed it. Ghostep is the ultimate expression of Phantom Blade Zero's combat philosophy: turning an enemy's lethal strike into your moment of glory.

### FAQ

**Can I parry a Killer Move if I have high Sha-Chi?**

No. Killer Moves cannot be parried regardless of your Sha-Chi level or guard status. The game's damage model treats them as unparryable attacks. Dodging is the only safe response.

**Does dying in Phantom Blade Zero cost days from the 66-day timer?**

Yes. Each death consumes one day from Soul's remaining lifespan. The exact interaction with the dodge system is that avoiding unnecessary deaths through proper dodge timing helps preserve your day count for the final confrontation.

**Are all enemies in Phantom Blade Zero vulnerable to Ghostep?**

Most enemies are, but some bosses have specific phases where Ghostep cannot be triggered. Check the boss-specific guide for details. The general rule is that any enemy using a Killer Move with a red indicator is a valid Ghostep target.
